# Fraud Pattern  
Scammers employ **impersonation** (e.g., posing as police, financial institutions) and **urgency-driven tactics** to extract sensitive information. They use **persuasive cues** (e.g., fabricated legitimacy, pressure) to lower victim suspicion during mid-stages of the scam. Key vulnerabilities include susceptibility to **persuasive cues** in Stage 3 and the lack of adaptive warnings to counter **escalating pressure** in later stages.

# Definition  
**SCRIPT-MIND** is a **cognitively adaptive LLM** trained to detect and mitigate scams by analyzing conversational patterns, psychological tactics, and contextual cues. It leverages **finetuned models** (e.g., EEVE-Korean-Instruct-10.8B, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) to improve detection accuracy and reduce misclassification of legitimate communications as scams.  

# Typical Attack Workflow  
1. **Impersonation of authority figures** (e.g., "This is the police; your account is under investigation").  
2. **Fabricated urgency** (e.g., "Your bank account will be frozen unless you transfer funds immediately").  
3. **Identity theft** through social engineering, exploiting personal details to gain trust.  
4. **Escalation tactics** in later stages, including deadlines, financial penalties, or threats to force compliance.  

# Behavioural Characteristics  
- **Initial suspicion** (Stages 1–2) declines sharply in **Stage 3** due to persuasive scammer cues.  
- **Recovery in suspicion** occurs in **Stage 4** as pressure and financial demands escalate.  
- **Stable relevance and anxiety** suggest victims may not fully recognize the scam until late stages.  

# Indicators  
- **Persuasive cues** in Stage 3 (e.g., fabricated evidence, threats) to reduce suspicion.  
- **Escalating pressure** in later stages (e.g., deadlines, financial penalties).  
- **Linguistic patterns** in high-risk phrases (e.g., "urgent legal action," "casual questions like 'Do you know Mr. XX?'" in impersonation scams).  

# Common Feature Patterns  
- **Role-playing authority** to mimic legitimacy.  
- **Tailoring messages** to exploit psychological vulnerabilities (e.g., fear of legal consequences).  
- **Use of urgency** to bypass rational decision-making.

# Fraud Pattern

# Executive Summary  
This document outlines a reinforcement learning (RL) approach to optimize fraud detection in transaction systems. The goal is to improve precision, recall, and Gross Merchandise Value (GMV) impact compared to baseline models by refining reward functions and iteratively adjusting detection workflows.

# Definition  
The system uses RL agents to identify and block high-risk transactions, balancing the trade-off between blocking fraudulent activity (True Positives) and minimizing false positives (blocking legitimate transactions) and false negatives (missing fraud).

# Detection Signals  
The reward function design includes:  
- **True Positive GMV**: `(action == 1) & (target == 1) * wgt`  
- **False Positive GMV**: `(action == 1) & (target == 0) * wgt`  
- **True Negative GMV**: `(action == 0) & (target == 0) * wgt`  
- **False Negative GMV**: `(action == 0) & (target == 1) * wgt`  

# False Positives  
Operational challenges include balancing Step 1 and Step 2 blocking actions to avoid excessive false positives (user friction) or false negatives (missed fraud). Key metrics include:  
- **Bad GMV Blocked Ratio**: Ratio of bad GMV blocked in Step 1 vs. Step 2.  
- **Total GMV Blocked Ratio**: Ratio of total GMV blocked in Step 1 vs. Step 2.  

# Prevention  
Mitigation strategies include:  
- **Iterative Reward Refinement**: Use reflection prompts to analyze failures and refine reward function parameters.  
- **Multi-Step Validation**: Ensure Step 1 and Step 2 blocking actions align with business goals (e.g., minimizing false positives in Step 1).  
- **LLM Prompt Engineering**: Leverage detailed prompts to guide LLMs in generating reward functions that balance precision, recall, and GMV impact.
